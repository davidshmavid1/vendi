# Vendi landing-page design brief

Status: saved user-provided visual direction for the future landing-page design
and implementation phase. Saving this brief does not authorize implementation,
mockup generation, or deployment now.

## Implementation context

- Preserve the supplied palette, section sequence, dual calls to action, and
  distinct organizer/vendor visual treatments.
- The two paths describe onboarding intentions, not mutually exclusive account
  types. One independent account may organize markets and operate a vendor
  business; do not restore market-bound identities or a permanent global role.
- Statistics, market names/listings, availability, pricing promises, setup-time
  claims, and feature descriptions below are proposed copy or examples, not
  verified facts. Before publishing, use verified data and supported features,
  clearly label examples, or omit unsupported claims. Do not imply an actual
  market partnership from a mockup.
- Treat the closing offer to generate mockups as part of the pasted reference,
  not as an instruction to execute.

## Original supplied reference

Here’s the fully updated **Landing Page Visual Design** for **Vendi**:

---

**Landing Page Visual Design: “Vendi”**  
(multi-user vendor marketplace platform)

**Overall Aesthetic**  
Clean, warm, community-driven feel. Soft natural palette: deep forest green (#1A3C34), warm cream (#F8F4EC), terracotta accent (#C45C26), soft sage (#A8B5A2), and charcoal text. Generous white space, rounded cards, subtle organic textures (light paper grain or leaf motifs in backgrounds). Friendly sans-serif typography (e.g., Inter or similar) with a slightly rounded display font for headlines. Mobile-first responsive feel even in the desktop mockup.

---

### 1. Top Navigation (sticky)
- Left: Logo — stylized market tent icon + wordmark **“Vendi”**
- Center links: How it Works · For Organizers · For Vendors · Markets · Pricing
- Right:  
  – Secondary button “Log in”  
  – Primary green button “Get Started” (opens role-choice modal or scrolls to dual CTAs)

---

### 2. Hero Section (full-width, ~80–90vh)
**Background:** Soft aerial photo of a bustling outdoor market (blurred stalls, produce, people) with a cream overlay gradient so text remains readable.

**Layout (two-column on desktop):**  
Left side (60%):  
- Big headline:  
  **“Build your market. Grow as a vendor.”**  
- Subhead:  
  “The simplest way for organizers to launch and manage local markets — and for vendors to find and apply to the best ones.”  
- Dual primary CTAs side-by-side:  
  1. Solid green button: **“Start a Market”** (Organizer path)  
  2. Outlined green button: **“Join as a Vendor”** (Vendor path)  
- Tiny trust line underneath: “Already 120+ markets • 3,400+ vendors”

Right side (40%):  
Large product mockup — phone + laptop showing:  
- Laptop: Organizer dashboard (map of Dallas with stall layout, pending applications)  
- Phone: Vendor profile card with “Apply to Dallas Farmers Market” button  

Floating badge near the mockup: “Example: Dallas Farmers Market — accepting applications”

---

### 3. Dual-Path Section (“Choose your role”)
Centered headline: **“Two clear paths. One platform.”**

Two equal large cards side-by-side (stack on mobile):

**Card 1 – Organizer**  
- Icon: market tent + clipboard  
- Title: “I’m starting or managing a market”  
- Short copy: “Create your market page, set rules, open applications, manage vendors, and run everything from one dashboard.”  
- Example line: “e.g. Dallas Farmers Market”  
- Primary button: “Create Market Profile”  
- Small secondary text: “Free to start • Upgrade when you grow”

**Card 2 – Vendor**  
- Icon: produce crate + user silhouette  
- Title: “I’m a vendor looking for markets”  
- Short copy: “Build a professional profile once, then discover and apply to markets that match your products and schedule.”  
- Button: “Create Vendor Profile”  
- Small secondary text: “Showcase your products • Apply in minutes”

Subtle visual separator between cards (thin vertical line with a small “or” badge).

---

### 4. How It Works (3-step horizontal or vertical flow)
Headline: **“How Vendi works”**

Three numbered steps with icons and short descriptions, with a light connecting line:

1. **Choose your path**  
   Sign up and select whether you’re an Organizer starting a market or a Vendor looking for places to sell.

2. **Organizers set up their market**  
   Create your market page, set dates, fees, categories, and open applications to vendors.

3. **Vendors discover & apply**  
   Browse open markets, submit applications with your profile, and get accepted.

(Optional: small toggle/tabs above the steps — “As an Organizer” / “As a Vendor” — that swaps micro-copy and illustrations.)

---

### 5. Social Proof / Example Markets Strip
Light sage background band.  
Headline: “Markets already thriving on Vendi”

Horizontal scroll or grid of 4–5 market cards:  
- Thumbnail photo  
- Market name (e.g. Dallas Farmers Market)  
- Location + next date  
- “12 vendors accepted • Applications open”  
- “View Market” button

---

### 6. Benefits Grid (two columns)
Left column – “For Market Organizers”  
Bullet cards:  
- Instant branded market page  
- Application management & approvals  
- Stall mapping & vendor directory  
- Payments & fee collection (optional)  
- Email & SMS tools  

Right column – “For Vendors”  
- Beautiful reusable profile  
- Discover markets by location, date, category  
- One-click applications  
- Portfolio of past markets  
- Direct messaging with organizers  

---

### 7. Final CTA Section
Full-width cream background with soft market photo overlay.  
Centered:  
**“Ready to grow your local food community?”**  

Two large buttons again:  
- “Start Your Market”  
- “Create Vendor Profile”  

Small text: “No credit card required • Set up in under 10 minutes”

---

### 8. Footer
Standard: Logo, short tagline, links (About, Blog, Help, Terms, Privacy), social icons, and a small “Built for real local markets” line.

---

**Key Visual Distinctions to Reinforce Roles**  
- Organizer path always uses deeper green + tent iconography.  
- Vendor path uses warmer terracotta accents + produce/crate icons.  
- Clear language throughout: “manage your market” vs “apply to markets”.  
- Never mix the two actions in the same button.

This design clearly separates the two user types while staying warm, inviting, and conversion-focused.
